Quest Construction — General Contracting

Ten directions for the new site

Each card below is a complete, working homepage — not a picture. Hover a preview and click once to scroll and interact with it, or open it full screen. They are deliberately not variations on one idea. 01–03 are the first set: angular colour planes with a cut‑out machine, a soft card‑built poster, and the flat brutalist one. 04–10 each take a named graphic‑design style and commit to it — Swiss, a bold colour field, Constructivist, clean utility, Art Deco, punk cut‑and‑paste, and industrial diagonal.

01

Site Plan

Layered · Editorial · Safest bet
Full screen
Click to interact

A hero built in real layers — faint engineering grid, a giant ghost wordmark, a hard‑edged accent plane with an angled boundary, and a cut‑out excavator sitting on top and crossing from the colour into the cream. No frames: the machine is a knocked‑out image, not a photo in a box. Below it the page drops to dark for the service cards and work grid, with a full accent figures band between them.

Best if you want depth and polish without shouting. This is the safe one.

02

Heavy Plant

Poster · Interlocked · Boldest
Full screen
Click to interact

Soft and card‑built. A dark navigation pill floats over the cream, the headline highlights its two payoff words in coloured pills, and the hero is a rounded plate of the crew on a finished slab. Beside it is the rack: one slat per trade we self‑perform, each a real photo under a flat tint — hover the slats and they open. Figures underneath, then the plant roster along the bottom.

Best if you want to be the boldest contractor in the valley.

03

Split Bay

Dark · Interactive · Most premium
Full screen
Click to interact

The only dark one. The hero splits down the middle — a solid accent panel on the left holding the headline and a short yard‑tour clip, a full‑height crane shot on the right with the figures laid across its base, and a slowly rotating stamp sitting right on the seam. Further down, the service names become giant list rows that summon their own photo to your cursor as you move over them.

Best if you want it to feel like a bigger, more expensive company than the competition. Worth opening full screen — the cursor effects do not show in a scaled preview.

04

Grid North

Swiss · Typographic · Most disciplined
Full screen
Click to interact

Swiss / International Typographic. One typeface at several weights on a visible twelve‑column grid, flush left, hairline rules instead of boxes, no rounded corners and no shadows. The hero is a full‑bleed photo wall — five jobsite photographs tiled with 2px gaps and run right to the viewport edges, the headline and the CTA occupying cells of the same grid. The figures row under it is justified to those same two edges so it closes on both; the nav bar keeps the page gutter. Services are numbered rows rather than cards, the credentials are a real data table, and the work is an index with a plate on every row.

Best if you want to read as precise and engineering‑led. The most restrained of the nine, and the one that ages slowest.

05

Ground Break

Poster · Green · Loudest
Full screen
Click to interact

A deep excavated‑earth green ground. The hero is full bleed: a jobsite fills the whole first screen, the giant wordmark is knocked over the top of it, and the figures run along the base as a hard bar. The figures bar is the last thing in the section: the photograph runs straight into the cream below it with no decorative shape in between. Below it alternates green and cream: chunky hard‑bordered service cards with one filled solid, then the cost guide on a cream plate.

Best if you want to be impossible to confuse with another contractor. The only one that is not black, white or cream.

06

Goop

Product-site soft · Rounded · Black hero
Full screen
Click to interact

Modern product‑site softness. The first screen is pure black: three jobsite stills cross‑dissolving in full colour under an inverted vignette, the headline centred over them, and the nav a white pill floating on top with the wordmark on a dark badge. Below the fold nothing is square — big‑radius bento cards on a pale tinted ground, dark rounded islands for the lead magnet and the contact band, and every control a pill. One geometric grotesk carries the whole page. Every pale ground is mixed from the accent, so swapping the accent above re‑tints the entire page, not just the buttons.

Best if you want to read as a modern product company rather than a contractor. The softest and most current of the set.

07

Bid Desk

Utility · White · Most lead‑driven
Full screen
Click to interact

Clean white and the most conversion‑shaped of the set. The hero photo is cut with a soft organic sweep along its bottom edge and a four‑field estimate bar straddles that edge — project type, where, budget, start date, then one accent button. Figures live in blob bubbles rather than boxes, and the work section has working filter chips.

Best if the site's job is to produce bid requests rather than to impress. Everything on the page points at one form.

Estimate barOrganic hero sweepBlob stat bubblesFilter chips
08

Machine Age

Art Deco · Symmetrical · Most premium
Full screen
Click to interact

Art Deco — the style of the era that actually built the skyscrapers. The hero is a hard split: a charcoal type column with the sunburst behind it, and a crane photograph running off the right edge, divided by a stepped ziggurat cut instead of a straight line. Strong verticals, ziggurat frames around every photograph, hairline double rules with a brass lozenge. Services sit inside three arches numbered in Roman, the figures run along a chevron‑divided rail, and the work is a row of stepped tiles.

Best if the high‑end custom‑home side is the business you want more of. Reads expensive without reading soft.

Ziggurat framesSunburstFive-panel friezeFramed reel
09

Site Notice

Punk · Xerox · Roughest
Full screen
Click to interact

Punk cut‑and‑paste. Every element is something physically stuck to a hoarding: flyers stapled up at angles, strips of tape, a rubber stamp, typewriter captions, and photographs blown out on a bad photocopier. Type is mixed on purpose. The lead magnet is a literal site notice with tear‑off phone tabs, and the work is a contact sheet.

Best if you want to look nothing like another contractor. The body copy still sits on clean paper, so it stays readable.

Xerox halftoneStapled flyersTear-off tabsContact sheet
10

Cross Cut

Industrial · Diagonal · Heavy plant
Full screen
Click to interact

Industrial B‑to‑B, the way heavy‑equipment and plant‑hire companies build a site. Every section boundary is a diagonal slash with an accent hairline riding it, photography is full‑bleed inside those slashes, and the machine is a cut‑out that breaks across the cut instead of sitting in a box. Graphite and steel, one accent, a 24/7 call‑out flash.

Best if the commercial and sitework side is the business you want more of — this is the one that looks like it owns its own plant. Proof is a wall of notched steel plates, services are a chevron list beside the excavator, and the work is a skewed filmstrip that opens on hover.
