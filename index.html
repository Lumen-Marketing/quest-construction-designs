Quest Construction — General Contracting

Seven directions for the new site

Each card below is a complete, working homepage — not a picture. Hover a preview and click once to scroll and interact with it, or open it full screen. They are deliberately not variations on one idea. 01–03 are the first set: angular colour planes with a cut‑out machine, a soft card‑built poster, and the flat brutalist one. 04–07 are the second set — all four cut their photography into organic shapes rather than boxes, and all four run the same proven homepage order.

How to use this page. Start with the three colour dots at the top — they change the accent on all seven designs at once, live, so you can settle the colour argument by eye instead of by description. Then use Desktop/Mobile to check how each one reshapes on a phone. All three accents are deliberately muted rather than full safety‑colour. Copy is placeholder throughout, and the photography is free stock we can swap for real Quest jobsite photos later.
What 04–07 share. Every one of them follows the same section order — logo and simple nav, a short headline with one stand‑out CTA, social proof, about, three offers, a lead magnet with a free cost guide, selected work, footer. Same skeleton, four different skins, so you are comparing the look rather than the layout.

01

Site Plan

Layered · Editorial · Safest bet

A hero built in real layers — faint engineering grid, a giant ghost wordmark, a hard‑edged accent plane with an angled boundary, and a cut‑out excavator sitting on top and crossing from the colour into the cream. No frames: the machine is a knocked‑out image, not a photo in a box. Below it the page drops to dark for the service cards and work grid, with a full accent figures band between them.

Best if you want depth and polish without shouting. This is the safe one.

Heavy Plant

Poster · Interlocked · Boldest

Soft and card‑built. A dark navigation pill floats over the cream, the headline highlights its two payoff words in coloured pills, and the hero is a rounded plate of the crew on a finished slab. Best if you want to be the boldest contractor in the valley.

03

Split Bay

Dark · Interactive · Most premium
Full screen

The only dark one. The hero splits down the middle — a solid accent panel on the left holding the headline and a short yard‑tour clip, a full‑height crane shot on the right with the figures laid across its base, and a slowly rotating stamp sitting right on the seam. Further down, the service names become giant list rows that summon their own photo to your cursor as you move over them.

Best if you want it to feel like a bigger, more expensive company than the competition. Worth opening full screen — the cursor effects do not show in a scaled preview.

04

Soft Site

Organic · Light · Most current
Full screen
Click to interact

Warm off‑white throughout. Every photograph is cut into an organic shape instead of a rectangle, and a giant accent blob sits behind the fold as a colour plane — but the type and the grid stay strict, which is what keeps it from going soft in the wrong way. Floating pill nav, oversized rounded offer cards with one filled in the accent, and a cost‑guide band before the work grid.

Best if you want to look like the most modern builder in the valley without going loud. The easiest of the four to live with.

Printed‑paper cream with a real grain over it, a soft optical serif at magazine scale, and lowercase headlines with the payoff word in italic. Bid Desk

Clean white and the most conversion‑shaped of the set. The hero photo is cut with a soft organic sweep along its bottom edge and a four‑field estimate bar straddles that edge — project type, where, budget, start date, then one accent button. Figures live in blob bubbles rather than boxes, and the work section has working filter chips.

Best if the site's job is to produce bid requests rather than to impress. Everything on the page points at one form.
