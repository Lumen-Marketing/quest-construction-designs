# Service-area jurisdiction verification

Every service-area page names the body that issues a building permit there. That is the
one claim on those pages a homeowner could act on and find wrong, so each one was checked
against the issuing body's own website or ordinance rather than inferred from the fact
that a place is incorporated.

**Checked 2026-08-29.** Jurisdictions change — San Tan Valley's changes on a known date
below — so re-check before any material relaunch.

What is verified here is **who issues the permit**. What is *not* verified is build
practice: housing stock, ground conditions and how Quest works. Those are written from
public knowledge of each place and Quest must cut anything that does not match how it
actually operates.

## City of Phoenix urban villages and informal areas

Laveen, Ahwatukee, Deer Valley, Camelback East Village and the areas people call North
and South Phoenix are **not municipalities**. Inside the city limits they permit through Phoenix.
Two boundaries matter and both are on the pages:

- **Gila River Indian Community** borders Laveen and South Phoenix to the south. It is
  sovereign, runs its own building safety, adopts its own code editions and requires
  third-party plan review. A parcel the wrong side of that line is not a Phoenix permit.
- **North Phoenix** is colloquial. Its northern fringe runs into unincorporated Maricopa
  County, where the permit is a county submittal.

**Deer Valley Airport Overlay** is a named district in the Phoenix zoning ordinance, not
an informal noise zone.

## San Tan Valley — changes on a known date

Incorporated **1 July 2026**, Arizona's 92nd municipality and the largest community ever
to incorporate in the state. Permitting is mid-handover:

- **Now, to end of 2026** — two steps: zoning clearance from the Town, then the building
  permit from **Pinal County** with that clearance attached, under an intergovernmental
  agreement.
- **During 2027** — the Town is expected to take permitting over in full.

**This page has a shelf life.** Re-check it in January 2027.

## Corrections this pass produced

Eight claims were wrong or too loose when first drafted, and all eight are fixed:

1. **San Tan Valley** — said only that the path should be confirmed. Now names the actual
   two-step and the handover date.
2. **South Phoenix** — claimed all of it permits through Phoenix. The Gila River Indian
   Community boundary makes that untrue.
3. **Laveen** — same boundary, same correction.
4. **North Phoenix** — claimed all of it permits through Phoenix. The northern fringe is
   unincorporated county.
5. **Deer Valley** — called the overlay an unnamed noise zone; it is the Deer Valley
   Airport Overlay district.
6. **Cave Creek** — implied the town handles septic and wells. Septic is Maricopa County
   Environmental Services; wells are the Arizona Department of Water Resources.
7. **Sun City** — missed the separate Sun City Fire District permit.
8. **Apache Junction** — described the county split as even. It is mostly Pinal.

## Also confirmed, and worth knowing

- **The client's own list filed Maricopa under Maricopa County.** The City of Maricopa is
  in **Pinal County**, about 35 miles south of Phoenix. The page names Pinal.
- **Queen Creek** is primarily Maricopa County but has extended into Pinal on its eastern
  and southern edges since the Ironwood Crossing annexation in 2018.
- **Apache Junction** spans both counties, most of it Pinal.

## Not verified: the demolition page's regulatory claims

The demolition service page, added 2026-09-01, makes four claims about how demolition is
regulated in the Valley. None of them was checked against a jurisdiction's own site the
way the permit-issuing bodies above were, and **Quest should confirm all four or cut
them** before they are quoted back at the company by a homeowner:

1. A demolition permit is a separate application from the building permit for whatever
   replaces the structure.
2. Cities want evidence of utility disconnection before issuing it.
3. Maricopa County wants a structure surveyed for asbestos before demolition.
4. Maricopa County is a federal non-attainment area for particulates, and larger sites
   need a dust control permit whose plan is enforceable.

The first two are how the trade generally works and the page states them as such. The
third and fourth name a specific regulator and are the two most worth checking, because
they are the two a reader could act on. The page deliberately does not quote a site-size
threshold, a permit fee or a processing time — those vary by city and by year, and a
stale number is worse than no number.
